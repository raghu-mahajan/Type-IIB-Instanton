1 Flat-space worldsheet conventions

On each worldsheet coordinate chart, the Euclidean worldsheet coordinates are denoted by z and z¯. Holomorphic fields depend on z, and anti-holomorphic fields depend on z¯. Target-space vector indices M,N take values in {0,1,…,9}. Repeated target-space indices are summed over this set. We use the ten-dimensional Minkowski metric

ηM⁢N:=diag⁡(−1,+1,…,+1). (1.1)

Indices on target-space vectors are raised and lowered with ηM⁢N and its inverse ηM⁢N. We set

α′=1. (1.2)

For two holomorphic operators A⁢(z) and B⁢(w), the notation A⁢(z)⁢B⁢(w)∼C⁢(z,w) means that, in the expansion valid for |z|>|w|, C⁢(z,w) contains precisely the terms that are singular as z→w. Write Singz→w⁡[A⁢(z)⁢B⁢(w)] for the sum of those singular terms. Every coincident-point product in these notes is understood to be normal ordered by point splitting. Thus the notation A⁢(w)⁢B⁢(w) means

A⁢(w)⁢B⁢(w):=limz→w(A⁢(z)⁢B⁢(w)−Singz→w⁡[A⁢(z)⁢B⁢(w)]). (1.3)

Normal-ordering colons are suppressed everywhere below. When a composite field is defined by an equation whose left-hand side is C⁢(z), every field on the right-hand side is evaluated at z unless a different argument is displayed explicitly. If T is a stress tensor, a holomorphic operator 𝒪h has conformal weight h when

T⁢(z)⁢𝒪h⁢(w)∼h⁢𝒪h⁢(w)(z−w)2+∂𝒪h⁢(w)z−w. (1.4)

The central charge c of T is defined so that the coefficient of (z−w)−4 in T⁢(z)⁢T⁢(w) is c/2. A field of Grassmann parity |A|∈{0,1} is called even when |A|=0 and odd when |A|=1; away from operator-product singularities, two such fields obey A⁢B=(−1)|A|⁢|B|⁢B⁢A. Derivatives without a bar mean ∂:=∂/∂z.

1.1 Conventions for the free boson

Let XM⁢(z,z¯) be the ten embedding coordinates. Their two-point operator product and its differentiated holomorphic form are

XM⁢(z,z¯)⁢XN⁢(w,w¯) ∼−ηM⁢N2⁢log⁡|z−w|2, (1.5)
∂XM⁢(z)⁢∂XN⁢(w) ∼−ηM⁢N2⁢(z−w)2. (1.6)

Equation (1.6) is the free-boson normalization used in Ref. [2]. The holomorphic stress tensor of the ten bosons is defined by

TX⁢(z):=−ηM⁢N⁢∂XM⁢∂XN⁢(z). (1.7)

With Eqs. (1.6) and (1.7), each XM has holomorphic conformal weight zero, each ∂XM has holomorphic conformal weight one, and the ten-boson central charge is

cX=10. (1.8)

For a target-space momentum kM∈ℝ1,9, define

k⋅X :=kM⁢XM, (1.9)
k2 :=ηM⁢N⁢kM⁢kN, (1.10)
Vk⁢(z,z¯) :=exp⁡(i⁢k⋅X⁢(z,z¯)). (1.11)

The holomorphic and anti-holomorphic conformal weights of Vk are

(hk,h¯k)=(k24,k24). (1.12)

Two useful consequences of Eq. (1.5) are

∂XM⁢(z)⁢Vk⁢(w,w¯) ∼−i⁢kM2⁢(z−w)⁢Vk⁢(w,w¯), (1.13)
Vk⁢(z,z¯)⁢Vp⁢(w,w¯) ∼|z−w|k⋅p⁢exp⁡(i⁢(k+p)⋅X⁢(w,w¯)), (1.14)

where k⋅p:=ηM⁢N⁢kM⁢pN.

1.2 Conventions for the free fermion

Let ψM⁢(z) and ψ~M⁢(z¯) be the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic components of the ten real worldsheet fermions. Their normalization is

ψM⁢(z)⁢ψN⁢(w)∼−ηM⁢N2⁢(z−w). (1.15)

The holomorphic fermion stress tensor is

Tψ⁢(z):=ηM⁢N⁢ψM⁢∂ψN⁢(z). (1.16)

Thus ψM has holomorphic conformal weight 1/2, and the ten-fermion central charge is

cψ=5. (1.17)

The free matter stress tensor and the matter supercurrent are defined by

Tm :=TX+Tψ, (1.18)
TF :=−ηM⁢N⁢ψM⁢∂XN. (1.19)

The minus sign in Eq. (1.19) is fixed jointly by Eqs. (1.6) and (1.15). It ensures that the picture-changing operator defined in Eq. (1.65) maps the −1-picture factor e−ϕ⁢ψM to ∂XM with positive coefficient. The superconformal operator products of Tm and TF are derived in Sec. 1.5.

We next fix the gamma matrices and the Ramond spin fields. Spinor indices α,β,γ take values in {1,…,16}. We use symmetric chiral gamma-matrix blocks

(ΓM)α⁢β=(ΓM)β⁢α,(ΓM)α⁢β=(ΓM)β⁢α. (1.20)

Their normalization is defined by

(ΓM)α⁢β(ΓN)β⁢γ+(ΓN)α⁢β(ΓM)β⁢γ=2ηM⁢Nδα.γ (1.21)

Equivalently, the 32×32 matrices

𝚪M:=(0(ΓM)α⁢β(ΓM)α⁢β0) (1.22)

satisfy

{𝚪M,𝚪N}=2⁢ηM⁢N⁢𝟏32. (1.23)

For p≥1, the antisymmetrized product is

𝚪M1⁢⋯⁢Mp:=𝚪[M1⁢⋯⁢𝚪Mp], (1.24)

where the brackets include the factor 1/p!. A symbol such as (ΓM1⁢⋯⁢Mp)αβ denotes the indicated chiral block of Eq. (1.24). We choose the target-space orientation and positive-chirality block by

ϵ01⁢⋯⁢9=+1,(Γ01⁢⋯⁢9)α=βδα.β (1.25)

Equations (1.20) and (1.25) are the ten-dimensional conventions of Ref. [1].

Let Sα⁢(z) and S~α⁢(z¯) denote the left- and right-moving positive-chirality Ramond spin fields. Type IIB means that these two fields have the same ten-dimensional chirality. Each has conformal weight 5/8 in its chiral sector. The symbols Sα and S~α denote the spin fields that occur in the −3/2 picture; the placement of the spinor index is part of this convention and is fixed by the operator products below, rather than by an unstated index-raising rule.

The Ramond operator products also contain the bosonized superghost ϕ. For use in the next three equations, ϕ is defined by

∂ϕ⁢(z)⁢∂ϕ⁢(w)∼−1(z−w)2,eq1⁢ϕ⁢(z)⁢eq2⁢ϕ⁢(w)∼(z−w)−q1⁢q2⁢e(q1+q2)⁢ϕ⁢(w). (1.26)

The complete β⁢γ bosonization is given in Sec. 1.4. We normalize the spin fields by the singular terms

e−ϕ⁢ψM⁢(z)⁢e−ϕ/2⁢Sα⁢(w) ∼i2⁢(z−w)⁢(ΓM)α⁢β⁢e−3⁢ϕ/2⁢Sβ⁢(w), (1.27)
e−3⁢ϕ/2⁢Sα⁢(z)⁢e−ϕ/2⁢Sβ⁢(w) ∼δαβ(z−w)2⁢e−2⁢ϕ⁢(w), (1.28)
e−ϕ/2⁢Sα⁢(z)⁢e−ϕ/2⁢Sβ⁢(w) ∼iz−w⁢(ΓM)α⁢β⁢e−ϕ⁢ψM⁢(w). (1.29)

The branch choices, phases, and factors in Eqs. (1.27)–(1.29) are those of Ref. [1]. The anti-holomorphic spin fields obey the same equations with every field and coordinate replaced by its anti-holomorphic counterpart. This identical left-right form is another place where the type IIB chirality choice enters.

1.3 Conventions for the b⁢c ghost system

The reparametrization ghosts b⁢(z) and c⁢(z) are Grassmann odd. Their holomorphic conformal weights and ghost numbers are

field h ghost number
b 2 −1
c −1 +1

Their singular operator product is

b⁢(z)⁢c⁢(w)∼1z−w. (1.30)

The b⁢c stress tensor is defined by

Tb⁢c:=−2⁢b⁢∂c−(∂b)⁢c, (1.31)

and its central charge is

cb⁢c=−26. (1.32)

On the complex plane, a nonzero b⁢c correlator contains three more c insertions than b insertions. We define the constant K by the normalization used in Ref. [2]:

⟨c⁢(z1)⁢c⁢(z2)⁢c⁢(z3)⟩b⁢c:=−K⁢(z1−z2)⁢(z2−z3)⁢(z1−z3). (1.33)

Here the subscript b⁢c means that the expectation value is taken only in the b⁢c sector. Equation (1.33) defines K; its relation to a D-brane or D-instanton tension additionally depends on the normalization of the string amplitude and is not assumed in these notes.

1.4 Conventions for the β⁢γ system

The superconformal ghosts β⁢(z) and γ⁢(z) are Grassmann even and have conformal weights 3/2 and −1/2, respectively. We bosonize them in terms of Grassmann-odd fields ξ,η and a Grassmann-even chiral scalar ϕ by

β :=∂ξ⁢e−ϕ, (1.34)
γ :=η⁢eϕ. (1.35)

The order of the factors in Eqs. (1.34) and (1.35) is part of the convention. The elementary operator products are

ξ⁢(z)⁢η⁢(w) ∼1z−w, (1.36)
∂ϕ⁢(z)⁢∂ϕ⁢(w) ∼−1(z−w)2, (1.37)
eq1⁢ϕ⁢(z)⁢eq2⁢ϕ⁢(w) ∼(z−w)−q1⁢q2⁢e(q1+q2)⁢ϕ⁢(w). (1.38)

It follows from Eqs. (1.34)–(1.38) that

β⁢(z)⁢γ⁢(w)∼−1z−w,γ⁢(z)⁢β⁢(w)∼1z−w. (1.39)

The bosonized stress tensors are

Tξ⁢η :=−η⁢∂ξ, (1.40)
Tϕ :=−12⁢(∂ϕ)2−∂2ϕ, (1.41)
Tβ⁢γ :=Tξ⁢η+Tϕ. (1.42)

In the unbosonized variables, the same stress tensor is

Tβ⁢γ=−32⁢β⁢∂γ−12⁢(∂β)⁢γ. (1.43)

Its central charge is

cβ⁢γ=11. (1.44)

The ghost number and picture number used below are additive charges defined on the elementary bosonized fields by the following table.

field β γ ξ η eq⁢ϕ
ghost number −1 +1 −1 +1 0
picture number 0 0 +1 −1 q

The conformal weight of the exponential is

h⁢[eq⁢ϕ]=−12⁢q⁢(q+2). (1.45)

The mode expansions of the weight-zero field ξ and the weight-one field η define

ξ0:=∮d⁢z2⁢π⁢i⁢z⁢ξ⁢(z),η0:=∮d⁢z2⁢π⁢i⁢η⁢(z). (1.46)

The large Hilbert space is the state space on which ξ0 acts without the restriction in Eq. (1.47). The small Hilbert space is the subspace of states |Ψ⟩ satisfying

η0⁢|Ψ⟩=0. (1.47)

Equations (1.34)–(1.47) follow the ordering, charges, and Hilbert-space convention of Ref. [2].

1.5 Conventions for the T⁢T and TF⁢TF operator products

Let T⁢(z) be the stress tensor of a holomorphic matter superconformal field theory, let TF⁢(z) be its supercurrent in the normalization defined below, and let cm be its central charge. We define the normalization of T and TF by the singular operator products

T⁢(z)⁢T⁢(w) ∼cm/2(z−w)4+2⁢T⁢(w)(z−w)2+∂T⁢(w)z−w, (1.48)
T⁢(z)⁢TF⁢(w) ∼3⁢TF⁢(w)2⁢(z−w)2+∂TF⁢(w)z−w, (1.49)
TF⁢(z)⁢TF⁢(w) ∼cm/6(z−w)3+T⁢(w)2⁢(z−w). (1.50)

Thus TF is one half of a supercurrent G normalized by G⁢(z)⁢G⁢(w)∼(2⁢cm/3)⁢(z−w)−3+2⁢T⁢(w)⁢(z−w)−1. This explicit algebraic relation is the definition of the relative normalization; no separate meaning is assigned to the phrase “normalized supercurrent.” Equations (1.48)–(1.50) match the convention used in Ref. [2].

For the ten free bosons and ten free fermions of Secs. 1.1 and 1.2,

cm=cX+cψ=15,T=Tm. (1.51)

The operator products therefore become

Tm⁢(z)⁢Tm⁢(w) ∼15/2(z−w)4+2⁢Tm⁢(w)(z−w)2+∂Tm⁢(w)z−w, (1.52)
Tm⁢(z)⁢TF⁢(w) ∼3⁢TF⁢(w)2⁢(z−w)2+∂TF⁢(w)z−w, (1.53)
TF⁢(z)⁢TF⁢(w) ∼5/2(z−w)3+Tm⁢(w)2⁢(z−w). (1.54)

For completeness, the coefficients in Eq. (1.54) can be reproduced directly. Substituting Eq. (1.19), the double contraction of the two fermions and the two differentiated bosons gives

(−ηM⁢N2⁢(z−w))⁢(−ηM⁢N2⁢(z−w)2)=104⁢(z−w)3=52⁢(z−w)3. (1.55)

The single fermion contraction gives

−12⁢(z−w)⁢∂XM⁢∂XM⁢(w)=TX⁢(w)2⁢(z−w). (1.56)

For the single boson contraction, expand ψM⁢(z)=ψM⁢(w)+(z−w)⁢∂ψM⁢(w)+𝒪⁢((z−w)2). The zeroth-order product ψM⁢ψM vanishes, while the first-order term gives

−12⁢(z−w)⁢(∂ψM)⁢ψM⁢(w)=Tψ⁢(w)2⁢(z−w). (1.57)

Adding Eqs. (1.55)–(1.57) gives Eq. (1.54) with Tm=TX+Tψ.

The total holomorphic stress tensor of the gauge-fixed critical theory is

Ttot:=Tm+Tb⁢c+Tβ⁢γ. (1.58)

Its central charge vanishes because

ctot=cm+cb⁢c+cβ⁢γ=15−26+11=0. (1.59)

1.6 Conventions for the BRST and PCO operators

The holomorphic BRST current is defined by

jB:=c⁢(Tm+Tξ⁢η+Tϕ)+b⁢c⁢∂c+γ⁢TF−14⁢γ2⁢b. (1.60)

In bosonized variables, the composite field γ2 is

γ2:=γ⁢γ=η⁢∂η⁢e2⁢ϕ. (1.61)

The BRST charge is the contour integral

QB:=∮d⁢z2⁢π⁢i⁢jB⁢(z), (1.62)

where the contour is counterclockwise around the operator on which QB acts. The balance cm=15 in Eq. (1.51) implies

QB2=0. (1.63)

The anti-holomorphic charge Q~B is defined by the tilded copy of Eqs. (1.60)–(1.62); the closed-string BRST charge is

Qclosed:=QB+Q~B. (1.64)

For two Grassmann-odd operators A and B, write {A,B}:=A⁢B+B⁢A. The holomorphic picture-changing operator (PCO) is defined in the large Hilbert space by

𝒳⁢(z) :=2⁢{QB,ξ⁢(z)} (1.65)
=2⁢c⁢∂ξ+2⁢eϕ⁢TF−12⁢[(∂η)⁢e2⁢ϕ⁢b+∂(η⁢e2⁢ϕ⁢b)]⁢(z). (1.66)

The factor of two in Eq. (1.65) is part of the convention adopted from Ref. [2]. Define Xcommon:={QB,ξ} to be the PCO without this factor. The two conventions obey

𝒳=2⁢Xcommon. (1.67)

The PCO 𝒳 has conformal weight zero, ghost number zero, picture number +1, and is BRST closed:

[QB,𝒳⁢(z)]=0. (1.68)

The anti-holomorphic PCO is

𝒳~⁢(z¯):=2⁢{Q~B,ξ~⁢(z¯)}. (1.69)

For an oriented worldsheet of genus g∈ℤ≥0 with b∈ℤ≥0 boundary components, define Ptot to be the sum of the picture numbers of all holomorphic and anti-holomorphic insertions, including PCOs. A nonzero correlator requires

Ptot=2⁢(2⁢g+b−2). (1.70)

For a closed worldsheet, b=0, this condition can be imposed separately as PL=PR=2⁢g−2. Equation (1.70) explains why PCO insertions are required when the picture numbers of the vertex operators do not already have the required sum.
